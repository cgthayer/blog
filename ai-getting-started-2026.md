In 2026, everyone uses AI to create code, but you may not have dug into learning about and building AI yourself. As a software engineer (or founder), it’s super tough to fit in learning when you have a full-time job, so here are some ideas to help you get started and dip a toe in the AI waters.

Pick just **one** that feels doable, and come back later.

See older: [[ai-getting-started]]

* Prompts: Go through a short (<2hrs) prompt engineering course like Coursera Prompts for Devs. There are lots of prompt engineering videos on YouTube as well (Tina Huang’s Prompt Engineering in 20 min)
* Video: Find a 15 min YouTube video AI overview you like (such as Tina Huang’s How To Self Study AI Fast)
* Search: When doing web searches for anything researchy or not super specific, use (https://you.com/, https://perplexity.ai) because these provide good citations.
* Game: Generate a simple game with claude artifacts or v0, e.g. “generate the game rocks, paper, scissors”. Ask it to make fixes or changes and have a look at the code.
* Project: If you can carve out 2–3 hours a week, come up with a fun project you think would be good try in python. Use Google Colab or jupyter notebooks to play around in python (free GPUs help). I really like Hugging Face’s smolagents for powerful but easy.
* Course: If you prefer a structured course that can be an hour or two over many weekends: Intro to AI Orchestration with LangChain and LlamaIndex is relatively short. You’ll find similar on https://udemy.com, and https://www.deeplearning.ai/ has lots of courses, short courses, and specializations
* Read: AI is a super broad area, and picking a project will help you focus on what you want to learn. I found Medium articles often helpful (like Towards AI), and Reddit discussions (many subs).
* News: Check on a source of news once a week, which can be whatever you find interesting. This could be a weekly mailing list, YouTube subscription, LinkedIn newsletter, etc. and these vary from generic industry news, to dev tools. I’m thinking of:
— YouTube channels: Mathew Berman Tech & Futurism, Skill Leap AI, Blazing Zebra, etc
— LinkedIn groups: AI, MLOps, and Robotics Newsletter, etc.
— Reddit subreddits: r/Rag, r/LLMDevs, r/LocalLLaMA, etc.
— Medium: Towards AI, Towards Data Science, etc.
* Research: If you do research or content work, and have local docs or PDFs to input, check out Google’s NotebookLM. Claude and others have similar tools now too. For verifiable responses use https://you.com/ and https://perplexity.ai which provide clear citations and references.

Alright, hope that’s useful. Please share additions, and let me know what worked and didn’t…
