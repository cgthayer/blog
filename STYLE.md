## Meta-Principles

These are the underlying motivations that shape every content and style decision.

- **Lighthouse, not gatekeeper.** The purpose of the blog is to cast light and share knowledge broadly — to help the AI engineering community navigate fast-moving terrain, not to position the author as an exclusive expert. Knowledge belongs to the community.
- **Courage and optimism.** The blog takes a constructive, forward-looking stance. Problems are named honestly, but the default posture is "here's what works today" and "here's where this is heading" — not "here's why everything is hard." Authentic, not pollyanna.
- **Systems thinking in everything.** The instinct is always to show how things connect — to reveal the system, not just the part. Diagrams, loops, and frameworks aren't decoration; they're how complex ideas become legible and shareable.
- **Open by default.** Community over private. Ideas are shared early, prototypes are shown rough, and readers are invited to improve on what's here. The LTS loop (Learn → Tinker → Share) is itself a build-measure-learn cycle, run in public.

## Audience & Purpose

**LTS (Learn, Tinker, Share)** is a technical blog aimed at experienced software engineers — typically 5+ years in, working at Series-A startups, primarily in Python — who are navigating the transition into AI and LLM application development. These are engineers who are capable and seasoned but are hitting unfamiliar territory: production reliability, prompt brittleness, cost control, and quality assurance in agentic systems. The purpose is threefold: to document real findings from hands-on experimentation (Learn), to prototype and validate ideas in practice (Tinker), and to publish those findings as actionable guides (Share) — helping peers avoid the pitfalls and fears that come with shipping AI systems in production, while building a personal brand and community around practical AI engineering.

## Writing Style

**Useful. Curious. Honest.**

- **Peer-to-peer, experience-first.** Posts are written as one experienced engineer sharing what they found — not as a teacher instructing students. The voice is direct and opinionated, but carries humility: "here's what I tried, here's what worked, here's where I was wrong." Readers are brought along, not talked down to.
- **Curious, opinionated, and open.** Posts stake a clear position and use metaphors and analogies to make hard ideas stick. But they invite pushback — AI is moving fast, today's best practice may be tomorrow's antipattern, and that's worth saying out loud. The default posture is constructive: here's what works today, here's what to watch.
- **Narrative arc, conversational tone.** Each post follows the LTS shape: idea → tinker → experiment → finding → share. The structure is there but worn lightly — it reads like a smart colleague walking you through their week, not a formal writeup.

### Post Structure

- **Opening:** frame the idea or question that sparked the exploration
- **Middle:** what was tried, what was learned, where it got interesting or broke
- **Closing:** the finding, stated plainly — plus a standing invitation to the reader to share their own experience

### Code & Examples

Code snippets are copy-paste runnable as-is — no pseudocode, no placeholders that require assembly. Use as much code as the article warrants; a post about embeddings might have several snippets, a conceptual post might have one or none. Link generously to repos, papers, and tools referenced — credit is part of the community ethos.

### What LTS Is Not

- Not a tutorial site — posts share findings, not step-by-step courses
- Not a news or commentary feed — model releases and AI headlines aren't the subject matter unless there's something worth tinkering with
- Not thought-leadership for its own sake — every post should leave the reader with something they can use or try

### Catchphrase & CTA

*Want to Learn? Let's Tinker and Share.*

Every post ends (or opens) with a standard call-to-action inviting readers to share their own findings — a comment, a reply, a correction. The community is the point.

## Visual Style

**Casual. Clear. Evolving.**

- **Rough, hand-drawn diagrams.** Visuals use Excalidraw with a sketch aesthetic — intentionally unpolished, like whiteboarding with a colleague. This signals that ideas are works in progress, open to revision, not handed down from on high.
- **Every visual has accompanying text.** Diagrams are never left to speak alone. A sentence or short paragraph always frames what the reader should take from the image, reinforcing the idea in a second modality.
- **Evolving visual language.** A shared library of shapes, colors, and conventions is being built over time. The goal is consistency across posts — same feel, same vocabulary of shapes — so diagrams are immediately recognizable as LTS. Until that library is mature, each new diagram should be checked against these questions:
  - Does it use the established color palette and hand-drawn style?
  - Are labels readable and minimal?
  - Does it simplify, not decorate?
